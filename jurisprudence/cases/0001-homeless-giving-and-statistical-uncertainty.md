# Case 0001 — Homeless Giving & Statistical Uncertainty  
Responsibility, Projection, and the Limits of Probabilistic Reasoning

---

## Scenario

An individual encounters a homeless man asking for money.

Variations:
- He states he is hungry.
- He asks money for medication.
- Physical signs suggest possible substance withdrawal.
- Statistical data suggests high substance abuse prevalence among homeless populations.

Question:

If money is given and later used for self-harm or indirect harm,
is the giver morally responsible?

---

## Core Ethical Tension

This case examines the boundary between:

- Statistical risk awareness
- Individual moral judgment
- Autonomy preservation
- Responsibility inflation

---

## Structural Risks Identified

### 1. Causal Inflation

Naïve reasoning may follow:

Money → substance → harm → giver responsible.

Extended forms:

Money → economy → printer → society → universal guilt.

Without clear boundary, responsibility becomes infinite.

A constitutional model must reject infinite causal regress.

---

### 2. Statistical Substitution Error

Statistical prevalence ≠ individual certainty.

Applying population-level probability to an individual as moral fact constitutes epistemic distortion.

Statistical awareness may increase caution.  
It may not replace individual moral evaluation.

---

### 3. Projection Risk

Assuming:
"Homeless = addict = harmful"

violates:

- Axiom 1 — Unity (reduction to category)
- 15 — Truthfulness (probability treated as certainty)
- Axiom 3 — Free Will (implicit determinism)

---

## Constitutional Mapping

### Unity  
Each person remains an individual moral agent.

### Free Will  
Past behavior or group statistics do not eliminate capacity for choice.

### Mutual Freedom  
Freedom may only be limited to prevent clear violation elsewhere.

Suspicion alone is insufficient.

### Truthfulness  
Uncertainty must be acknowledged explicitly.

### Means = Ends  
Compassion must not become disguised control.

---

## Proportional Decision Framework

The Constitution does not require:

- Giving money.
- Refusing money.
- Solving systemic poverty.
- Assuming deception.
- Assuming honesty.

It requires:

- No projection as certainty.
- No guilt-driven reaction.
- No contempt.
- No manipulation.
- No responsibility inflation.

---

## Legitimate Responses

Constitutionally aligned actions may include:

- Giving money from clarity without projection.
- Offering food instead of cash.
- Offering to purchase medication directly.
- Respectful refusal.
- Limited engagement without humiliation.

If statistical risk is high (e.g., 90% likelihood of substance use),
providing direct sustenance rather than cash may be proportionate.

This is not punishment.
It is calibrated caution without identity-based condemnation.

---

## Key Distinction

Statistical information may influence:

- Risk awareness
- Degree of caution

It may not determine:

- Moral blame
- Identity-based restriction
- Preemptive condemnation

---

## Core Insight

This case is not about generosity.

It is about:

- The boundary of responsibility
- The misuse of statistics
- Protection against causal inflation
- Respecting autonomy under uncertainty

There is no perfect answer.

There are only proportionate responses free from distortion.
