# Constitutional Governance Model  
Universal Constitution 2.0

---

## 1. Structural Layers

The framework consists of three distinct layers:

---

### Layer 1 — Canon (Immutable)

Includes:

- Axioms
- Core Law
- Universal Laws
- Practical Principles
- Four-Line Summary
- Revision Note

This layer remains unchanged in the canonical edition.

---

### Layer 2 — Interpretative Jurisprudence (Governed Evolution)

This layer documents:

- Case analyses
- Edge-case resolutions
- Conflict-of-freedom examples
- Clarified applications of principles
- Precedent decisions

Interpretations must:

1. Be explicitly traceable to specific canonical articles.
2. Not contradict or weaken any axiom.
3. Survive Mutual Freedom and Means = Ends consistency analysis.
4. Be documented with transparent reasoning.
5. Be human-reviewable.

Interpretations do not modify the Canon.

They demonstrate its correct application.

Official interpretations require approval by designated Canon Guardian(s).

---

### Layer 3 — Implementation & Engineering

Includes:

- AI alignment architectures
- Constraint frameworks
- Pseudocode
- Audit models
- Validation test cases
- Regression precedent sets

This layer may evolve.

It must:

- Preserve Canon Layer integrity.
- Maintain axioms as hard constraints.
- Log reasoning trace.
- Remain audit transparent.

---

## 2. Derived Rule Policy

AI systems may generate derived operational rules.

A derived rule is valid only if:

1. It is logically consistent with the Canon.
2. It does not redefine or weaken axioms.
3. It passes Mutual Freedom conflict testing.
4. It passes Means = Ends integrity testing.
5. It is externally reviewable.
6. It is documented with full reasoning trace.

Derived rules do not amend the Canon.

They are classified as applications.

---

## 3. Constitutional Audit Requirement

All AI decisions must log:

- Activated axioms
- Invoked laws
- Conflict analysis
- Harm probability assessment
- Intervention threshold reasoning
- Means–Ends verification

Audit logs must remain human-readable.

---

## 4. Guardian Model

Canon Guardians are responsible for:

- Protecting canonical integrity
- Reviewing official interpretations
- Preventing silent weakening of constraints
- Preserving structural coherence

Guardianship protects structure — not personal authority.

---

## 5. Principle of Hard Constraints

Axioms function as non-optimizable constraints.

They may not be overridden for:

- Efficiency
- Utility maximization
- Statistical gain
- Risk reduction
- Long-term benefit projection

If a decision violates a hard constraint, it is invalid.
